# Radio research, round 1 (2026-11-23)

Notes from pages read for docs/plans/research-radio.md, shelf A (The Twilight Zone Radio Dramas). Facts are as each page
states them. "Not stated" means the page does not say.

## Radio World, "Radio Enters the Twilight Zone" (18 September 2002)
URL: https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/radio-enters-the-twilight-zone (byline: RadioWorld)
- Stacy Keach hosts. Produced by Falcon Picture Group LLC, "which penned a licensing deal with CBS Enterprises".
- Dennis Etchison was hired to adapt Serling's TV scripts for radio.
- "One hundred fifty 'Twilight Zone' radio dramas are in the works", for cassette and CD sale.
- Syndicated by Dick Brescia Associates to "hundreds of commercial radio stations and The American Forces Radio &
  Television Service".
- The original Twilight Zone scores are used for the theme and background music; The Hollywood Edge supplies sound effects.
- Executive producer Carl Amari; series producer Richard Wolski.

## The Great Detectives of Old Time Radio, "Audio Drama Review: The Twilight Zone Radio Dramas, Volume 1"
URL: https://www.greatdetectives.net/detectives/audio-dramas-twilight-zone-radio-dramas-volume-1/
- No reviewer named, no article date (earliest reader comment 15 June 2015).
- Volume 1 (Audible) collects six stories: "Night Call" (Mariette Hartley), "Long Live Walter Jameson" (Lou Diamond
  Phillips), "The Lateness of the Hour" (Jane Seymour), "The 30-Fathom Grave", "The Man in the Bottle" (Ed Begley Jr.),
  "The Night of the Meek" (Chris McDonald).
- Verdict: "Overall, this is a good collection with some good audio quality, some solid soundscapes, and mostly
  well-done musical production."

## BBC Radio 4 Extra (2016 onward): broadcasts of the Falcon series, not a new BBC production
- Gizmodo, Cheryl Eddy, "The BBC Is Bringing Back The Twilight Zone As a Radio Drama" (22 February 2016) and Nerdist,
  Kyle Anderson (23 February 2016) report ten 40-minute episodes on Radio 4 Extra from March 2016, starting with "An
  Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge", with Stacy Keach narrating; Gizmodo says "Producer Carl Amari has owned the rights
  since 2002."
- The BBC Programme Index pages read (series 3 and 4) all credit Falcon Picture Group and Carl Amari, and say the
  series "was first adapted by American producer Carl Amari in 2002 as a 40-minute radio drama series". So the BBC run
  is the Falcon series broadcast in Britain; the record radio/twilight-zone-radio-dramas-bbc is filed correctly.
- The Programme Index pages list the FULL CAST and credits for each episode, e.g.:
  - Series 3, ep. 3 "Static" (b074bs88): stars Stan Freberg, with Ann Whitney, David Darlowe, Ellie Weingardt, Bernie
    Landis; adapted by Dennis Etchison, written by Charles Beaumont from a story by Oceo Ritch; directed and produced by
    Carl Amari.
  - Series 3, ep. 7 "Caesar and Me" (b077nvqq): Jason Alexander, with Zanny Laird, Meg Thalken, Rich Komenich, Lisa
    Joyce; written by Adele Strassfield, adapted by Dennis Etchison; produced and directed by Carl Amari and "Roger
    Wolkski" (as printed; Radio World gives series producer Richard Wolski).
  - Series 4, ep. 5 "Probe 7, Over and Out" (b08hrb5f): Lou Gossett Jr., Tony Mockus Sr, Sam Derrence, Oksana
    Fedunyszyn; written by Rod Serling, adapted by Dennis Etchison; produced and directed by Carl Amari and JoBe Cerny.
  - Series 4, ep. 7 "Nothing in the Dark" (b08kbj6y): Marshall Allman, Roslyn Alexander, Jeff Morrow, Doug James, Mike
    Starr; written by George Clayton Johnson, adapted by "MJ Eliot" (M. J. Elliott).
- The index pages give no broadcast dates and say the schedule information "may not be accurate". The cast lists are
  the best source found so far for Q7 (full cast). Next: find the Programme Index pages for every episode.

## Wikipedia, "The Twilight Zone (radio series)", read directly 2026-11-23
- Table saved as wikipedia-episode-table.tsv (176 rows: 17 blocks of 10 plus 6). The article's blocks match our
  `season` and `season_order` on all 176 records, and the star matches `voiced_by` on all but two, where we list a
  second actor too. Our `volume` and `volume_order` follow a different (release) numbering and were not checked here.
- Adapters corrected from it and the BBC: "Nothing in the Dark" is M. J. Elliott's adaptation (we had Etchison);
  "The New Exhibit" is Etchison and JoBe Cerny.
- The article lists 81 episodes as Blu-ray extras and 30 on DVD (our series record said 82).
- Director: the infobox says Roger Wolski, the text says Carl Amari produced and directed. The BBC listings credit
  Carl Amari, sometimes with Roger Wolski or JoBe Cerny. Left as is.

## Original radio stories: 16, as the series record says
- The Twilight Zone Vortex ("A Dimension of Sound", JP, 19 March 2017) labels 16 episodes "An original radio play",
  all from volume 16 on. Our 17 included "Free Dirt", which the Vortex lists as "Based on the story by Charles
  Beaumont" (the BBC listing calls it "Charles Beaumont's TV script"). Free Dirt is now on neither new shelf.
- Unmade scripts: the Vortex calls "Gentlemen, Be Seated", "Pattern for Doomsday" and "Who Am I?" unproduced
  teleplays. "The Time Element" is "Based on a teleplay by Rod Serling": it was produced (Desilu Playhouse, 1958), so
  it came off the unmade-scripts shelf.
