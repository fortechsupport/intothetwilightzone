# Television research round 3: sources for fan-noted homages (2026-11-21)

Twenty fan-noted television homages (sourced only to fan wikis) were checked against their English Wikipedia episode
articles. A missing mention on Wikipedia does not disprove a homage, so nothing was removed or downgraded.

Changed:
- The Simpsons, "Bart's Comet" → "The Shelter": now confirmed by its makers (David Mirkin's DVD commentary, cited by
  Wikipedia). New connection to "The Monsters Are Due on Maple Street" from the same statement.
- The Simpsons, "El Viaje Misterioso de Nuestro Jomer" → "The Old Man in the Cave": now documented (the official
  episode guide, Richmond and Coffman 1997, pp. 220–221, cited by Wikipedia).
- The Simpsons, "The Strong Arms of the Ma" → "Time Enough at Last": Wikipedia source added; it gives no citation, so it stays fan-noted.
- Futurama, "Benderama": Wikipedia source added (calls it part of Futurama's ongoing Twilight Zone parody).

For Adam:
- Treehouse of Horror IV: Wikipedia ties "Terror at 5½ Feet" to "Nightmare at 20,000 Feet" (already on file, documented)
  and calls "The Devil and Homer Simpson" a parody of "The Devil and Daniel Webster". Our links from that segment to
  "Escape Clause" and "One for the Angels" rest only on the Twilight Zone fan wiki.

No mention on Wikipedia (still fan-noted): Marge on the Lam, Them Robot, Homie the Clown, Wild Barts Can't Be Broken,
New Kids on the Blecch, Futurama's Mother's Day, Anthology of Interest I, A Clockwork Origin, Lrrreconcilable
Ndndifferences, Seinfeld's The Van Buren Boys and The Cigar Store Indian, Masters of Horror's Valerie on the Stairs.
No standalone article: Buffy's Older and Far Away, Family Guy's Wasted Talent, Angel's Peace Out.
