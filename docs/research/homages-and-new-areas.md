## 1. The 149 unconfirmed (fan-noted) links

Each was searched for a maker's statement or a published source. **10 upgraded**, **3 doubtful**, **1 tied to a different episode**, **135 still unconfirmed**. Every checked link now says what was found in its text ("Checked 2026-11-20: ..."), so Adam can see why it stayed unconfirmed.

**Upgraded to documented (a published source makes the link):**

- `bridesmaids--nightmare-at-20-000-feet`: Westword's 'Top Ten Twilight Zone References in Pop Culture' lists the Bridesmaids airplane scene: Wiig's medicated, paranoid passenger is likened to Shatner in Nightmare at 20,000 Feet, with a colonial woman churning butter in place of the gremlin.
- `futurama-spanish-fry--twilight-zone-1959`: SYFY Wire's piece on the Scary Door (quoting writer J. Stewart Burns on its origin as a TZ tip of the hat) states that in Spanish Fry a Scary Door segment plays over the end credits.
- `medium-still-life--the-midnight-sun`: A Start TV feature on Medium's 3-D episode 'Still Life' says the Serling intro recorded for 'The Midnight Sun' was reused, with impressionist Mark Silverman voicing the new narration.
- `the-jack-benny-program-twilight-zone-sketch--twilight-zone-1959`: Slashfilm describes the January 15, 1963 episode: Serling plays himself, then plays 'Mayor Twilight Zone' in a fog-bound sketch where Rochester doesn't know Benny, and he closes with a joke about Benny's perpetual age of 39. It says CBS used the appearance to promote the show's return that month.
- `payback--the-jeopardy-room`: In a 2009 io9/Gizmodo feature, Lauren Davis says Payback's ending 'draws directly from' 'The Jeopardy Room'. This is the critic's own claim; no statement from the filmmakers was found.
- `the-simpsons-the-monkeys-paw--a-small-talent-for-war`: In Syfy Wire's rundown of every TZ parody in Treehouse of Horror (James Grebey, Aug 2024), the segment is called a partial parody: mostly W. W. Jacobs's story, with a Kang and Kodos invasion of a disarmed Earth borrowed from 'A Small Talent for War'.
- `the-x-files--twilight-zone-1959`: Westword's list of top Twilight Zone references in pop culture says The X-Files named Senator Richard Matheson after the TZ writer, and adds the joke that the show did not go as far as a 'President Rod Serling'.
- `spongebob-squarepants-the-tidal-zone-recurring-gag--twilight-zone-1959`: TV Insider describes The Tidal Zone as a one-hour crossover special of four vignettes with characters from SpongeBob and its spinoffs, opening with a spoof of Serling's introduction. Wikipedia has a page for the special.
- `the-woke-zone-the-babylon-bee--twilight-zone-1959`: The American Spectator ran a piece introducing the Babylon Bee's 'The Woke Zone' shorts, and the Daily Wire also covered a Woke Zone 'Twilight Zone episode', so the TZ-spoof framing is reported beyond TV Tropes.
- `saturday-night-live-twilite-zone-sketch-paul-simon-george-harrison--twilight-zone-1959`: The A.V. Club's retro review of the Nov 20, 1976 Paul Simon/George Harrison episode singles out a funny Twilight Zone parody in which Dan Aykroyd does an uncannily accurate Rod Serling.

**Doubtful (marked low confidence, kept for Adam to decide):**

- `austin-powers-international-man-of-mystery--number-12-looks-just-like-you`: Wikipedia's Dr. Evil article traces the pinky gesture to Lorne Michaels (Dana Carvey's claim). Myers credited Blofeld in You Only Live Twice as the main model. It says nothing about The Twilight Zone.
- `twilight-zone-golden-earring--twilight-zone-1959`: American Songwriter reports that George Kooymans wrote the song with spy fiction in mind, specifically Robert Ludlum's Bourne novels, and says the title and lyrics have no tie to the TV series.
- `toy-story-five-characters-in-search-of-an-exit`: No source found, and Pixar's documented origins (the Tin Toy short and Lasseter's own toys) don't involve TZ.

**Different episode:** The Truman Show is compared by critics to the 1989 episode "Special Service", not "A World of Difference". A new link to Special Service was added (unconfirmed: a comparison, not stated inspiration); the old one stays, marked low confidence.

**Kept as unconfirmed but with a known different origin** (worth a look; consider excluding or relabelling as a comparison): The Good Place (Schur cites Sartre's No Exit), Shadow in the Cloud (WWII gremlin folklore), M3GAN (writer cites Magic and Child's Play), Dead Silence (dummy modelled on Charlie McCarthy), A Nightmare on Elm Street (news reports of deaths in sleep), The Invisible (remake of a Swedish film), Unknown (from the novel Hors de moi), Vivarium (grew from the short Foxes).
