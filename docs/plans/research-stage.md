## 1. Where Stage stands today

There are 19 records on two shelves: Theatre (15) and Live readings (4). Only one is official: Anne Washburn's play.

What is wrong with it:
- **The shelves mix three different things.** Productions of Washburn's licensed play, stagings of the original
  teleplays by community and college companies, and original parodies all sit on Theatre. Examples: the Dark Room
  "Twilight Zone Parody Series" (San Francisco) and Impro Theatre's "Twilight Zone Unscripted".
- **Live readings is misnamed.** Three of its four records are full stagings: Hopewell Theatre, Blue Room Theatre
  (Chico) and Theater Schmeater (Seattle).
- **Washburn's play is one record.** The Almeida premiere (2017), the West End transfer (2019) and the Citadel
  Theatre production (2024–25) are not linked as one play with several productions.
- **No shelf for Serling's own work on stage.** Stage versions of his teleplays (Requiem for a Heavyweight, Patterns
  and others) are leads to check in research; none is recorded yet.
- **Thin sourcing.** Several records rest on one listing page. One script collection names no adapter or publisher:
  "Rod Serling's Stories from the Zone".
- **Not yet searched:** Night Gallery and the revival series on stage.

## 2. Structure: five shelves

Each shelf is A to Z, with official and unofficial items apart.

| Shelf | What goes on it | Official? |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The Twilight Zone, the play** | Anne Washburn's adaptation as one play page. It lists every production (Almeida 2017, West End 2019, Citadel 2024–25, and any others found) with dates, venue, director and cast. It also names which episodes the play draws on. | Official (licensed) |
| **B. Stagings of the episodes** | Productions that perform the original teleplays: professional, community, college and public-broadcaster stagings (WSKG's "Twilight Zone Live", 2009). Each is marked licensed only where a source says so. | By licence |

| **E. Parodies and tributes on stage** | Original comic or improvised shows built on the show's format. Examples: the Dark Room series, Return to the Twilight Zone (Lafayette), Twilight Zone Unscripted, and The Twylight Zone: The 6th Dimension. They also list on Discover > Legacy > Parodies. | Unofficial |

Theme-park shows stay in Visit > Places > Attractions; Stage links to them.

## 3. On every page

**A production or reading:**
- **Header:** type, the company and city, the year, and the Official or Unofficial label.
- **Facts:** dates (opening and closing), the venue linked to Places, the company linked to Organizations, the
  episodes or teleplays performed (each linked), the adapter, director and principal cast linked to People, and
  the licence where a source states it.
- **About:** the production in our own words.
- **Media**, all link-only: reviews by named critic and publication, production photos on the company's own page,
  the programme, and trailers.

**The play page (A):**
- What the play is and which stories it weaves together.
- A list of every production in date order: venue, director, cast.
- Reviews by critic and publication.
- Who licenses it for performance, where a source says so.

## 6. Sources and fetch budget

- The theatres' own pages: Almeida, Official London Theatre and Citadel Theatre.
- Reviews in the national press: The Guardian, The Telegraph, The Stage, Variety.
- **Licensing house listings:** for Washburn's play and any published acting editions of the teleplays. These name
  the licensor and the publisher.
- Local press for community stagings, which already source most of shelf B.
- Wikipedia for Serling's stage adaptations (shelf C), checked against a second source.
- BroadwayWorld and Playbill listings, as leads that need a second source.

**Budget:** about 60 fetches in one round, under an hour at the shared 100 an hour. The Movies round 3 leftovers
(trailers and home video for Serling's other films) add about 15.

## 7. Blessing-ready

- **Accuracy a rights holder would trust:** every production has dates and a venue from the theatre, the company
  or the press. "Licensed" appears only where a source says so.
- **Respect for their property:**
  - No photos, programmes or recordings are hosted.
  - Unlicensed stagings and parodies are marked unofficial.
  - Nothing implies CBS or the Serling family endorsed a production unless a source says so.
- **Honoring the people:** Serling's own work on stage is shown as part of his career. Washburn, the directors
  and the casts are credited by name.
- **Credit where credit is due:** every company, adapter, director, principal actor, photographer and critic is
  named, and so is each source's author and publication.
- **Sending fans to official sources:** current productions link to the theatre's own page, with no ticket resellers
  and no affiliate codes.
- **No money:** no ads, no affiliate links, no sponsorships.

## Decided here (Adam reviews later)
- Live readings that are really full stagings move to shelf B (Hopewell Theatre, Blue Room Theatre, Theater
  Schmeater).
- Theme-park shows stay in Places > Attractions.
- **Research round (2026-11-24), what the rights holders say:** the Rod Serling Memorial Foundation's own answers
  (rodserling.com FAQ) say "CBS owns all Twilight Zone rights. Not the Serlings, not the Foundation", that staging an
  episode needs CBS's permission, that "The Serling family can grant permission for a reading only, but it usually
  must be free to the public", and that Concord Theatricals handles Requiem for a Heavyweight and Patterns. So, under
  Q2, a staging of an episode is official only where a source says CBS gave permission (the 2023 Larchmont audio
  reading, "special permission from CBS/Paramount"). Hopewell Theatre's 2025 "permission from the estate" is recorded
  in those words and stays unofficial. Washburn's play is official: Den of Geek reports the producers won the rights
  from CBS and "courted the blessing" of the Serling, Beaumont and Matheson estates.
- Shelf C productions are marked official as Serling's own work (section 2), and each one says "licence not stated"
  unless a source says more. The Broadway Requiem rests on IBDB's "Written by Rod Serling". The same rule makes
  both readings of Serling's unmade pilot script "The Happy Place" (2019 and 2025) official; the Paley Center's 2009
  reading of "The Masks", a CBS episode, stays unofficial because no source states a licence.
- Records with only a listing (TheaterMania, a ticket page or the producer's own news page) wait for a second source
  under Q1 and are listed below, not recorded: Theatre West's 2005 "The Twilight Zone: Live on Stage" (Los Angeles,
  "Will the Real Martian Please Stand Up?" and "The Four of Us Are Dying"; it mentions a 2002 version directed by
  Walter Koenig), "A Fifth Dimension: An Unauthorized Twilight Zone Parody" (Kraine Theater, New York, 2017), Adams
  State University's 2020 student staging, the RiverTree Theatre Requiem (Westword gives no date or city) and the
  Southern Tier Actors Read Patterns reading (no year on the page).
- Subtypes set for the structure step: washburn-production, episode-staging, serling-play, live-reading,
  radio-style, parody and tribute-show. Until then Stage keeps its two current shelves, with readings and radio-style
  shows on Live readings.

- **Structure step (2026-11-24):** the five shelves are the-play, episode-stagings, serling-on-stage (new),
  live-readings and parodies, placed by subtype; the old Theatre address redirects to Stagings of the episodes.
  Shelf C groups by play; Live readings groups Readings and Radio-style shows; parodies also list in Legacy >
  Parodies, tributes (Twilight Zone Unscripted, Red Barn) only in Stage. Shelves list A to Z like every other
  shelf; on the landing they show in alphabetical order.
- The play page keeps its address (/films/the-twilight-zone-stage-play/): its stories, its productions in date order
  and links to production pages (the Citadel record).
